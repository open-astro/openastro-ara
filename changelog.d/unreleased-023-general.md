### Added
- **Fault history retention.** The §42.5 fault log was append-only for the daemon's lifetime. A new Settings → Storage knob, **Keep fault history (days)** (`storage.fault_log_retention_days`, default 90, 0 keeps everything), and a daily sweep in the daemon (two minutes after start, then every 24 h) delete fault rows detected longer ago than that. (#1145)
- **Target by coordinates on the Plan screen.** The Planning search bar has a pin button (and a pasted position in the search box opens it) for a target typed as RA/Dec: SIMBAD/Gaia sexagesimal or decimal forms, a J2000 / JNow choice, a name, a live read-back of the J2000 position the run will carry, and a recent-targets list that persists on the device. "Add to plan" builds the same imaging run a Tonight's Sky row does, so the sequence slews there (the daemon precesses to the mount's own epoch); "Show on sky" frames it on the planetarium. JNow entries are converted to J2000 in the client (precession, nutation, aberration; checked against astropy to under 0.5″). Part of #1267.
- **Live tab exposure timer.** A timer in the right rail, under Take One, shows the exposures the daemon runs (sequence frames, Smart Focus probes, plate-solve captures; a Take One shows its progress on its own card, and Live View has no timer) with the frame type, filter, elapsed / total clock and a progress bar, switching to *Downloading* when the shutter closes. Backed by new `camera.exposure_started` / `camera.exposure_complete` / `camera.exposure_failed` WebSocket events from the daemon's shared capture core.
- **PHD2-style guide graph.** Guiding moved from the Live tab's right rail to a strip along the bottom of the screen that behaves like PHD2's graph window: scrolling RA / Dec error traces with the correction pulses as bars, dither markers and shaded settle windows, star-lost marks, PHD2's controls (frames in view, y range, arc-sec / pixels, corrections, Clear) and its stats over the frames in view (RMS RA / Dec / Total, peaks, RA oscillation index). Fed by new per-frame `guider.step` and per-session-event `guider.event` WebSocket events. The RMS now reads in arcseconds only when a real pixel scale is known (the guider's, or the guide focal length + pixel size in Settings) and in guide-camera pixels otherwise — the old rail panel labelled the daemon's pixel figure with ″.
- **Guide exposure in Tune Guiding.** Imaging → Guiding → Tune Guiding (the settings button) now has a Guide exposure picker (the guider's own list, 10 ms to 30 s; "Unset" leaves the guider's duration alone). It is saved in the profile (`phd2.guide_exposure_ms`), pushed with the guider's `set_exposure` on connect and on Apply, and takes effect on the next guide frame. A choice outside the dark-library range is flagged in the dialog.
- **Rotate camera by hand, on the Plan screen.** Rigs without a rotator turn the camera while framing: the **Rotate camera** panel docks beside the sky, measures the camera against the framing dial's angle with a chosen exposure — one frame at a time (**Single**: shoot, turn, shoot again) or continuously (**Loop**) — shows the delta still to turn, the solved frame with north and the planned framing drawn over it, and draws where the camera really points on the sky beside the planned box. Readout frames (Single and Loop) run binned (the camera's largest, up to 4×4) on a correspondingly short exposure so they keep up with your hands; **Done** then takes one 1×1 frame at the full plate-solve exposure and approves the framing (or says how far off it still is). The run no longer pauses on a *Rotate camera by hand* step. (#1266, reworked)
- **Live run status.** The run band shows the current instruction, the target and a live frame counter (`frames_captured`); frames file under the target's name instead of "Imaging". (#1266)
- **Guide-camera focus knows when it is done.** The guide-camera card computes the HFR the guide optics can reach (seeing and diffraction), says **In focus** at or under it, compares 3-frame medians for the trend, and stops the live loop once focus holds. (#1266)
- **Setup → Smart Focus: focus the main telescope and the guide camera from one place.** A new step in the Setup checklist, between Connect equipment and Polar align. The main-telescope card runs autofocus and shows the whole run as it happens — every probe on a live V-curve with the fitted curve and the chosen best position, the statistics (measured HFR at best focus, stars, fit quality, probes kept, duration, temperature, filter) and a picture of the focused field with star rings — and can cancel a run, including one a sequence started. The guide-camera card is a live focus readout for the hand-turned helical focuser on a guide scope or off-axis guider: a live view of the guide camera with HFR, star count, peak and FWHM, an HFR trend and a best-so-far to beat, with frames borrowed through the guider so Ara never opens the guide camera itself. An off-axis guider shares the main scope's focus, so that card waits until the main telescope has been focused this session (or you say it already is). Connecting the guider's own camera as the main camera while the guider is connected is now refused with an explanation, since two programs driving one sensor is what broke guiding. New daemon routes: `GET/POST /api/v1/autofocus/{state,frame,cancel}` and `/api/v1/equipment/guider/focus/*`; new events `autofocus.step_complete`, `autofocus.curve_fit`, `autofocus.completed`, `autofocus.failed`.

### Changed
- **Small follow-ups from the accepted watch-list (#1185).** The profile-share import advisory lists every category the export actually strips (custom horizon, optics geometry, camera electronics, filter list, guide-scope geometry, tracking and sky-model calibrations were missing). A repeated sky-catalog query is answered from memory until the catalog file changes. The four `calibration.dark_library.*` WS event names that nothing ever published are gone from the catalog and the playbook.
- **Sky-data re-downloads honour ETags (#1171).** A package install records the server's `ETag` beside its `Last-Modified`, and a re-download sends both as `If-None-Match` / `If-Modified-Since`, so a catalog re-uploaded byte-for-byte with a fresh date is still a 304 and nothing is fetched. The playbook now states the Data Manager's download mechanics (daemon-side, conditional, no `Range` resume and why).
- **Audit hygiene (#1174).** NuGet versions moved to one `Directory.Packages.props` (Central Package Management) for all seventeen projects, so no two projects can drift on the same package; the CI NuGet cache key includes it. The ported device layers' warning and error toasts (the static `Notifier`, ~45 call sites in Equipment, Sequencer, PlateSolving and Profile) now land in the §46 notification inbox as Equipment warnings/errors — PHD2's "settle timed out", the dome radius misconfiguration, a mount that will not dither — deduplicated over five minutes, instead of reaching only the daemon log. `dotnet list package --deprecated/--vulnerable` is clean apart from xunit 2.9.3 ("legacy", not vulnerable), which stays until the solution moves to the new test runner; the reason is recorded next to the version.
- **Bench / CI hygiene (#1173).** The Sanity job installs gojq, the engine behind `gh --jq`, so the Flutter-bump selector cross-check runs on the engine production uses. The weekly Flutter bump's "already proposed" guard ignores a fork PR of the same branch name (the #997 failure mode) and is pinned by a test. Three bench tests that held a fixed 5 s / 600 ms / 500 ms now count refresh ticks or device reads instead, so a loaded runner stretches the wait rather than the claim. A native `linux-arm64` bench lane (`Bench (linux-arm64, native)`, hosted arm64 runner, no QEMU) runs the `bench` test category on every server change — the platform the Pi deployment targets.
- **Guider connect handshake (#1156).** Each post-handshake step (profiles, twin, engine push, equipment connect, lock-shift params, pixel scale) is now independently best-effort: a step that fails logs and the rest still run. Equipment connect/disconnect RPCs wait 15 s instead of 60 s; `DisconnectPHD2Equipment` honours the caller's cancellation.
- **Server test suite runs in parallel and in seconds, not minutes (#1265).** Fixtures now run on parallel workers (`[assembly: Parallelizable(ParallelScope.Fixtures)]`, worker count = core count) and the device services expose an internal refresh-cadence knob, so bench fixtures that used to sit through multiples of the 2 s production refresh interval, a 5 s switch settle window or a 6 s filter-wheel slot wait tick every 100 ms instead. The one test that waited out the mount's ten-minute slew-settle bound now scripts the device to refuse the goto (the scripted Alpaca device can answer PUTs); the stall path it used to reach keeps its own test, which shrinks the mount's settle bound through an internal `SlewSettleMaxPolls` knob. The fake guider answers `get_app_state` with `Stopped` so a profile push no longer pays two 10 s StopCapture timeouts. Fixtures that touch disk, loopback HTTP or a simulator carry `[Category("IO")]`; `dotnet test OpenAstroAra.Test --filter "TestCategory!=IO&TestCategory!=Integration"` is the quick unit run. Full suite on a laptop: 18 min → under 30 s; CI's 4-core runner proportionally.
- **Start Guiding parks the run instead of imaging unguided.** When guiding will not start, the run pauses awaiting you with the guider's reason, and Resume retries. Sequences built by hand or imported now park there too, where the instruction used to fail (and follow its error behaviour). Start Guiding first takes the guide camera back from a running live-focus loop and releases a polar-align lease nothing holds. (#1266)
- **Runs are cooked to the rig.** A run built from a target plan adds guiding only with a guider, filter switches only with a filter wheel, autofocus only with a focuser and the rotate-by-hand step only without a rotator. (#1266)
- **Five-shot Smart Focus.** A first shot that already reads in focus is checked with one shot either side, a shot at the fitted vertex and a confirmation frame before it counts; a bracket that does not confirm runs the full sweep. "In focus" is judged against the HFR the sweep measured, with a 10 % tolerance (was 5 % of a re-fitted minimum). The drawn V is fitted through the run's own shots. (#1266)
- **Polar Align redesigned for the field.** The running screen is now three cards: the guide camera's live frame, an **Alignment** card (bullseye, the total error as one large figure, and Altitude / Azimuth each with a bold "↑ Raise" / "← Move west" direction), and an **Alignment quality** card that explains the error for people who don't think in arcminutes: arcseconds from the pole, a verdict (Excellent / Very good / Good / Rough / Far off), worst-case star drift over a 5-minute exposure, and a Moon-width comparison. The alignment figures read at arm's length; buttons and body text match the rest of the app. Exposure and Loop/Single (Take Frame) sit in a bottom bar and apply to the next frame. Under night mode's red filter the readouts switch to full brightness, since the zone colours would otherwise be the dimmest things on screen. (#1259)
- **CI builds Android and iOS.** The native-build matrix now compiles a release APK and an unsigned iOS build, and checks that the committed launcher icons match `icon_sources/`. (#1129)

### Fixed
- **Centering on a second target while one is running is refused, not silently merged.** A second centre request for other coordinates used to join the running centering job and report that target as centred. It now answers 409 naming the running job (cancel it with `DELETE /api/v1/jobs/{id}` first); a repeat request for the same coordinates still joins the running job. (#1149)
- **Centering on a mount whose coordinate frame has not been read yet.** In the window after a connect and before the first refresh read the mount's equatorial system, the position the centering loop used for its offset was labelled JNOW by guess, so a J2000 mount's first iteration carried the ~0.36° precession error into the offset (it self-corrected on the next pass). The position read now resolves the frame first (one bounded read per connection, like the slew's). A sequence Stop during a centering sync no longer waits out that read. Polar alignment reports a seed slew the mount refused as "slew failed" rather than an internal error. (#1222, merged in #1293; the entry was left out of that PR)
- **Plate-solver output no longer writes the run checkpoint per line.** Every line a solver prints reaches the run's progress sink, and each report wrote the §28 checkpoint file (a serialize plus a move on the SD card); a chatty solver did hundreds of writes per centering. Progress-driven checkpoint writes are now rate-limited to one per second with a trailing write so the freshest state always lands; start, pause, resume and stop still write at once. (#1219)
- **Plate solving: an interrupted database download is no longer picked.** A star database with a single tile file left by an interrupted download (next to a complete D80, say) counted as installed and was named for narrow fields, so ASTAP failed with exit 33; a database now needs at least two tiles (ASTAP's single-file databases are exempt), incomplete ones are logged once and left out of the Settings list. The solver reads the database directory once per change instead of twice per solve. Saving a solver path of `/usr/bin/astap` now shows the stored `/usr/bin/astap_cli` straight away and re-reads the database status, and a solver configured as a URL gets a boot warning that says so instead of "binary not found at https://…". (#1215)
- **A mount reconnect only counts as recovered once a read succeeds.** When AlpacaBridge stayed latched after the reconnect, the mount answered Connected while every read still failed, so the recovery ladder resumed the paused run and posted "Telescope recovered" with the mount unusable. "Recovered" now waits for one clean refresh tick; a reconnect that did not clear the latch walks the next rung and ends in the usual "not recovered" notice and terminal action. A reconnect now sends the old connection's disconnect before the new connect (the ordered pair that clears the bridge) instead of racing them. The latch is recognised on any failed read, not only the first, so a driver that always refuses an unrelated property no longer hides it or holds the episode open. When the Slewing read is latched too, a goto the daemon saw start holds the trip, for at most five minutes. Two consequences: connecting the same mount again while its session is still latched now really reconnects (it used to be a no-op, so the ladder's later tries could never send the clearing pair), and the hour-long watch that re-adopts a lost device after the ladder gives up no longer takes a latched mount for "back" on Connected alone; it keeps re-dispatching the connect until a read succeeds. (#1246)
- **Guider on another machine: sharper host check, bounded DNS, one verdict per recovery.** A profile host like `raspberrypi.site2` was judged to be this machine by its first name label alone, so a second SBC with the same name got the local guider service started or restarted; the name check now needs a matching domain (or `.local` / router-style suffixes) and otherwise lets DNS decide. The DNS lookup is bounded by the caller, so a guider restart request answers within ~1.5 s even where the resolver ignores cancellation. A recovery pass decides locality once instead of on every reconnect attempt. The remote-guider "Guider connection lost" notice is a Warning like the local one, and a remote guider that never returns leaves the same diagnostic a local failure does. Switching to another profile that uses the same guider no longer abandons an auto-reconnect in progress. (#1234)
- **Direction-pad speeds are the ones both mount axes honour as given.** The speed chips were built from the E/W axis's rate bands clipped to the N/S axis's slowest and fastest rate, which stopped the N/S refusal but not a mismatch inside that window: on a mount whose N/S axis offers a few fixed steps, a 5 % chip ran E/W at the picked speed and N/S at the nearest step, 6–12× slower on a diagonal press. The pad now offers only the rates that lie inside a band on both axes, so neither axis snaps a chip. A mount whose N/S rates read late (the first capabilities pass saw a driver error) now has its speeds re-published once they arrive, instead of keeping the unclipped list for the session. (#1230)
- **Every guide-camera drop is on the record.** When the guide camera flapped during one guider session, only the first drop got a fault-log row (the §42.2 reaction is deliberately one-shot per episode, and the row was written on the same path). Each later drop now gets its own row, with no action stamp since nothing was done, and the camera's next reconnect resolves it. The reaction stays one-shot. (#1241)
- **Reconnect Equipment on the guider waits one retry timeout, and a Stop cancels it.** The sequencer's guider connect could wait almost two retry timeouts when the daemon's own recovery pass ended without reconnecting late in the window (the fallback connect started a fresh window). It now fixes one deadline at entry. A sequence Stop/Abort during that wait cancels it at once instead of waiting it out (Connect Equipment passes its token to mediators that take one), the grace timer is released as soon as the recovery pass finishes, Disconnect Equipment racing a daemon shutdown no longer throws, and the guider's info now reports that PHD2 can shift the lock position. (#1228)
- **DUT1 past the bundled table continues smoothly.** The IERS long-term formula is now anchored to the snapshot's last value, so crossing the table's end no longer steps UT1-UTC by ~26 ms; the last table day counts as covered (the extrapolation starts at the next midnight, not after the last midnight); a pathological `OPENASTROARA_DUT1_TABLE` value falls back to the embedded table instead of poisoning every later transform; the raw `finals2000A` parser is pinned by a byte-exact excerpt of the real file; CI warns when the snapshot is within 90 days of its end (`scripts/check-dut1-freshness.py`). (#1232)
- **Plate-solve RA error near 0h.** A solve whose field straddled 0h reported the RA separation the long way round (a ~24h figure, shown as `00:00:00`), so the RA error and RA pixel error were wrong for targets near the equinox. The separation is now folded to ±12h. A declination that wraps through zero in a FITS `OBJCTDEC` card now carries its `+` sign. (#1217)
- **Tune Guiding → Apply no longer stops guiding.** Apply used the full profile push, which also re-sends the guider's equipment selections; the guider only accepts those with its equipment disconnected, so every Apply ran `stop_capture` + `set_connected(false)` and ended the guide session (seen 2026-10-04 while changing aggressiveness mid-run). Apply now asks for the tuning-only push (`POST /api/v1/equipment/guider/profile/push?scope=tuning`: aggressiveness, minimum move, dec guide mode, guide exposure), which leaves the equipment connected; `guider.profile_pushed` carries `scope: "tuning"`.
- **Sequences no longer skip every target.** The altitude conditions were not registered, so their check failed and every target block was skipped; they are registered now (and skipped only when the astrometry libraries are missing). (#1266)
- **The Imaging viewer shows a sequence's frames**, not only Take One's. (#1266)
- **A planetarium search after a named target keeps the searched object's name** (a run framed on NGC 7000 was called "Moon"). (#1266)
- **Autofocus probes run at the profile's gain and offset.** They used whatever gain the camera was at, which on a fresh connection fitted noise; a noise-flooded probe is now dropped. The classic sweep also ignores far-wing probes that saw too few stars. (#1266)
- **Guide-camera live focus decodes frames on the Pi** (it needed a Windows-only library and gave up after five frames). (#1266)
- **Session frame counts are kept.** `sessions.frame_count` stayed 0; it now counts frames as they land and stays true after a library move or delete. (#1266)
- **Autofocus finds focus from far away.** A start a few hundred steps out left the stars as large faint discs the full-resolution detector barely saw, so the sweep aborted as "untrustworthy". Every run now begins with a coarse search on an 8× software-binned frame: it probes the start and ±(Steps × Step size) (3 extra exposures and moves on a routine refocus that is already close), walks toward smaller stars with doubling steps when focus is elsewhere, and centres the fine sweep there. The fine sweep skips probes too defocused to measure instead of aborting, fails only when fewer than half are usable, and re-centres up to twice when the best point sits at an edge. Every probe stays inside the focuser's reported travel (and never below 0). A search that finds no stars, or cannot bracket focus within its 20-probe walk (plus up to 6 refinement probes), fails with a clear message. Verified on the production rig: from 640 steps out it landed within a few steps of the best probe. (#1260)
- **Polar alignment no longer fails to seed when the mount starts well off the pole.** The two-point seed used the solved RA separation as the rotation about the mount's axis; near the pole those differ, so with the axis a few degrees off every seed was rejected as `axis_fit_failed`. The rotation now comes from the mount's own reported RA before and after the seed slew. Found and verified on the production rig, which then reached 0.5′. A seed slew the mount accepts without turning now fails as `slew_failed`. Seed frames' WebSocket events carry the per-attempt id (`seed-a-1`, `seed-b-1`, …) on both `polar_align.frame_started` and `frame_complete`, where `frame_complete` and `last_frame_id` used the bare `seed-a` / `seed-b`. (#1259)
- **The app scans for your rig every time instead of remembering an address.** It used to save the rig's IP address and reconnect to it, so a rig that moved (backyard to a remote site, a new DHCP lease, Ethernet to Wi-Fi) left the app stuck on "your rig didn't answer" with no way out. Nothing about the rig is stored on the device any more — every launch scans the local network and lists every rig it finds, and you pick yours (it never picks for you, since a site can have several). Once connected, a dropped link still reconnects to that rig on its own. Addresses saved by older versions are deleted on first launch. A **Choose a different rig** button on the profile screen goes back to the scan list. (#1129)
- **Android finds rigs on the network.** mDNS discovery never worked on Android (the socket option it used is refused there, and the app never took Android's multicast lock); both are fixed, verified on a Pixel Tablet against a live rig. (#1129)
- **Small client fixes found on a Pixel and an iPad:** the rig list shows the rig's name instead of its full service name; the profile screen no longer says "Connected to" a rig that isn't answering; the live-update connection no longer logs an "Uncaught error" on every retry; connection errors say "this iPhone or iPad" / "this Android device" instead of "this computer"; the iOS Local Network banner points at the right Settings path; the profile card scrolls instead of overflowing on short screens. (#1129)
- **Update rollback follow-ups (found on the Pi).** A pushed update that was rolled back left the rejected package (~70 MB) in the root-only rollback folder; it is now removed. The package's runtime paths use `/run` instead of the legacy `/var/run`, so installing no longer prints systemd-tmpfiles warnings. (#1122)
