### Breaking changes
- **The Polar Aligner's Stop route answers 409 when the device does not confirm the Stop** (#2069): `POST /equipment/polaraligner/stop` now waits up to 3 s for the device to report both axes still, sending Stop up to three times, and answers 409 (`stop_not_confirmed`) when it does not; `POST /equipment/polaraligner/home` and `POST /equipment/polaralign/correction/resume` answer 409 until a later Stop is confirmed, and a Polar Align run whose end cannot confirm the Polar Aligner stopped ends `failed` with `polar_align.error` `aligner_did_not_stop`. Emergency Stop then lists `stop_polar_aligner` among its failed steps. **After upgrading:** a client or script that stops the Polar Aligner treats a 409 as "the motors may still turn" and sends Stop again (or cuts the device's power); an older app shows the new stop causes as "Ara stopped the motors."
- **A Polar Align run with a Polar Aligner stops at 40 moves or at a travel cap** (#2069): a run that needed more than 40 moves, or took an axis further than three times its first measured offset plus 30′, used to carry on; it now stops the motors with `move_limit` or `travel_limit`. **After upgrading:** finish such an alignment by hand, or start again from a pose closer to the pole.

### Fixed (polar alignment)
- **The Polar Aligner's Stop is checked, its moves are followed, and a run is bounded** (#2069, #2102): every Stop of the motorised Polar Aligner (the correction Stop, Polar Align's Stop and every end of a run, the Stop route, Emergency Stop and the safety and fault parks) now waits for the device to report both axes still, and sends Stop again up to three times in 3 s. When it cannot confirm it, the app says so ("Ara sent Stop, but the Polar Aligner didn't confirm its motors stopped") and offers Stop again in place of Resume, Ara sends Stop again at each measurement, and nothing moves until it is confirmed. A Polar Align run whose end cannot confirm the Polar Aligner stopped ends failed (`aligner_did_not_stop`) and tells you to cut its power, instead of showing "stopped". A device that answers Stop with an error (a driver without Stop) and then reads still counts as stopped, so it does not block later runs. When the device reports its position, a move that goes the wrong way, or much too far or too short, stops the motors before the next move. A run keeps each axis within a travel cap and sends at most 40 moves, and a device that cannot stop a move is sent at most 10′ at a time. The wrong-way and wrong-size messages now say "A move on…" (they can come from any move, not only the test move). A test now covers the aligner's own check that refuses to move a device that cannot move while the mount tracks.
