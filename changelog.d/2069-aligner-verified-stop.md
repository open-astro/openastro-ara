### Breaking changes
- **The Polar Aligner's Stop route answers 409 when the device does not confirm the Stop** (#2069): `POST /equipment/polaraligner/stop` now waits up to 3 s for the device to report both axes still, sending Stop up to three times, and answers 409 (`stop_not_confirmed`) when it does not; `POST /equipment/polaraligner/home` and `POST /equipment/polaralign/correction/resume` answer 409 until a later Stop is confirmed, and a Polar Align run whose end cannot confirm the Polar Aligner stopped ends `failed` with `polar_align.error` `aligner_did_not_stop`. Emergency Stop then lists `stop_polar_aligner` among its failed steps. **After upgrading:** a client or script that stops the Polar Aligner treats a 409 as "the motors may still turn" and sends Stop again (or cuts the device's power); an older app shows the new stop causes as "Ara stopped the motors."
- **A Polar Align run with a Polar Aligner stops at 40 moves or at a travel cap** (#2069): a run that needed more than 40 moves, or took an axis further than three times its first measured offset plus 30′, used to carry on; it now stops the motors with `move_limit` or `travel_limit`. **After upgrading:** finish such an alignment by hand, or start again from a pose closer to the pole.

### Fixed (polar alignment)
- **The Polar Aligner's Stop is checked, its moves are followed, and a run is bounded** (#2069, #2102): every Stop of the motorised Polar Aligner (the correction Stop, Polar Align's Stop and every end of a run, the Stop route, Emergency Stop and the safety and fault parks) now waits for the device to report both axes still, and sends Stop again up to three times in 3 s. When it cannot confirm it, the app says so ("Ara sent Stop, but the Polar Aligner didn't confirm its motors stopped") and offers Stop again in place of Resume, Ara sends Stop again at each measurement, and nothing moves until it is confirmed. When a Stop left owed from before a run is confirmed only partway through that run, the run says so ("The Polar Aligner has now confirmed its motors stopped. Ara won't move them again until you Resume.") and waits for Resume instead of moving the device again by itself. The app's Stop button for an unconfirmed Stop now reaches the Polar Aligner even before the run has read it, and after a lost link and a reconnect, and its confirmation lets you Resume. A slow link no longer stretches a Polar Aligner Stop past its 3 s window, so Emergency Stop's Polar Aligner step stays inside its time limit. A run that took more than 15 s to wind down and only then found its Polar Aligner stop unconfirmed now still tells you. A Polar Align run whose end cannot confirm the Polar Aligner stopped ends failed (`aligner_did_not_stop`) and tells you to cut its power, instead of showing "stopped". A device that answers Stop with an error (a driver without Stop) and then reads still counts as stopped, so it does not block later runs. When the device reports its position, a move that goes the wrong way, or much too far or too short, stops the motors before the next move. A run keeps each axis within a travel cap and sends at most 40 moves, and a device that cannot stop a move is sent at most 10′ at a time. The wrong-way and wrong-size messages now say "A move on…" (they can come from any move, not only the test move). A test now covers the aligner's own check that refuses to move a device that cannot move while the mount tracks. A Stop left owed from before a polar alignment check is sent again when the check ends, and the check fails (`aligner_did_not_stop`) instead of giving its verdict when that Stop is not confirmed. An owed Stop now belongs to the Polar Aligner it was sent to: a different Polar Aligner connected in its place can be homed and driven, while the same device reconnecting still owes it. With no Polar Aligner connected (its power cut, its Switch or mount disconnected), a Stop it owes no longer fails or stops Polar Align runs, and Home says no Polar Aligner is connected; the device owes the Stop again if it comes back. On a device that does not take up its own backlash, the first move after an axis turns the other way may fall short like the axis's first move, without stopping the motors as a wrong-size move. When a Stop that got through still leaves an axis moving, the message says so instead of repeating an earlier send's error. A Polar Aligner driver that fails a Stop with an unexpected error now counts as an unconfirmed Stop (owed, and told) instead of breaking the run's end.
